# Deviations, implementation choices and extensions

Each entry states what the paper did (or did not specify), what this replication
does, and why. "Gap n" refers to the numbered list of unspecified details in
`nanopubs/drafts/00_paper_summary.md`. The code that implements each choice is
named so the entry can be checked against it.

## Extension (not a deviation): probability calibration

The paper sums uncalibrated RF vote fractions trained on 50/50 samples ("expected
area"). Those scores are not probabilities at the true regrowth prevalence. We
additionally report (`03_analysis.py`):

- prevalence π = exact area of Fagan regrowth pixels / exact area of the sampling
  domain (class-0 ∪ class-1 masks), from the 30 m tile sums of `02b`;
- prior-shift correction p′ = π·p / (π·p + (1 − π)(1 − p)) applied to the scores,
  with expected area Σ p′ × area and area with p′ > 0.5;
- cross-check: isotonic calibration fitted on a random half of the validation set
  with case weights that reweight the balanced set to prevalence π (equivalent in
  expectation to drawing at prevalence π); never used in training;
- Brier score and reliability diagram on the other (evaluation) half, weighted
  the same way.

## Testing

Every rule was smoke-tested end to end (`snakemake --config smoke=1`: 1° tiles in
-75…-73°E, 4…6°N; 3,000 points per class; 100 trees; outputs in
`data/clean_smoke/`, `results/smoke/`, `figures/smoke/`) before the full run. The
smoke test also exercised the MCD64A1 fallback (2 months, tile h10v08) and the
Earthdata `~/.netrc` login from a detached (`setsid`) process.

## Diagnostic 3 — transferability (training scope)

**Question.** Our step-2 model (Colombia only) gives an uncalibrated expected
area of 4.0 Mha against 10.3 Mha for the authors' pantropical map in the same
domain. On Colombian non-regrowth validation points, the authors' map scores
p > 0.5 on 52.6 % (mean 0.52) and ours on 6–8 % (mean 0.17–0.19). Leading
hypothesis: training scope. Diagnostic 3 trains **the same model specification**
on a Neotropical sample and tests transfer to Colombia (`02c`, `03d`). This is a
diagnostic extension, not part of the paper's method.

## Area of applicability (AOA) — extension

Diagnostic extension, not part of the paper's method (`03e_area_of_applicability.py`).
It is a Python port of CAST `trainDI()` / `aoa()` (Meyer & Pebesma 2021, MEE,
doi:10.1111/2041-210X.13650). The source read was github.com/HannaMeyer/CAST at
commit `779e13f`, files `R/trainDI.R`, `R/aoa.R`, `R/aoa-helpers.R` and
`R/caret-helpers.R`, plus caret's `rf` `varImp` module.

| # | CAST | This port | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Factors become one dummy per training level (`caret::dummyVars`, not full rank). A level that is not in the training data scores 0 on every dummy. Each dummy gets the **full** weight of its variable. | We do the same for `lc_2000` (`lc_2015` at prediction) and `biome`. They are integer-coded in our data, so they are declared categorical explicitly. The importance weight is not split across the dummies. | Follows CAST exactly. Note that `scale()` gives rare levels (e.g. LC class 10, with 3 training records) very large scaled values, so those points have high DI. This is CAST behaviour. |
| A2 | `scale()`: training mean and sample sd. Weights multiply the scaled columns. Euclidean distance. | Same (`ddof=1`). Zero-variance columns are dropped with a message (CAST would stop). This can only happen in the smoke test. | — |
| A3 | Weights = `caret::varImp(model, scale = FALSE)`. For an `rf` classifier this is the mean over classes of the per-class `randomForest::importance()` columns: OOB permutation mean decrease in accuracy, divided by its standard error (randomForest's default `scale = TRUE`). Negative weights are set to 0. | sklearn has no OOB permutation importance. We use the permutation decrease in **balanced accuracy** (= mean over the two classes of the per-class accuracy decrease), not divided by a standard error, on a balanced hold-out of 10,000 records drawn from each model's training pool outside its training draw. We use 5 repeats and set negative values to 0. | This is the closest available equivalent. The SE scaling of randomForest is not reproduced, so the relative weights can differ. |
| A4 | Training DI: distance to the nearest training point in the CV-training part of the fold that holds the point out, divided by the mean, over training points, of each point's mean distance to all other training points. Folds come from the caret model. | Our models are not fitted by CV. Folds are 5-fold `GroupKFold` by HEALPix cell, depth **6** (~100 km; NESTED, `healpix_geo`, `ellipsoid="WGS84"`), on each model's own training draw. The same depth is used in step 3(b) and diagnostic 3. Sensitivity thresholds use depth 7, random 5-fold, and no CV (`useCV = FALSE`). | CAST recommends spatial folds when the model is assessed with spatial CV. |
| A5 | Threshold = min(Q3 + 1.5 IQR, max) of the training DI (`.di_threshold`, R `quantile` type 7). New data are inside the AOA if DI ≤ threshold. | Same (numpy `linear` quantiles = type 7). | — |
| A6 | Nearest neighbour by brute force (`FNN`, chunked). | Exact `scipy.spatial.cKDTree`, in batches of 500,000 query points. The mean pairwise distance is computed exactly in chunks. | Same result, faster. |
| A7 | — | Applied to the step-2 Colombia model and to the diagnostic-3 Neotropical models (without and with Colombia), all refitted exactly as in `03` / `03d`. New data: the 1-in-100 Colombian grid (2018/2015 inputs, exact WGS84 areas) and the Colombian validation points (2000 inputs; 2018 inputs for the non-regrowth comparison). The authors' map is only **split by our AOA**; their AOA cannot be computed without their training data. | — |
| A8 | Meyer & Pebesma relate DI to CV error. | Accuracy is reported by DI decile for (i) the Colombian validation points (for the Neotropical models, these are the transfer points) and (ii) CV predictions on each training draw with the same depth-6 folds: one fold model per fold, with the same settings and seed `SEED + fold`. Accuracy per class and **balanced accuracy** are reported next to accuracy. The share of regrowth points falls steeply with DI (97 % in the lowest decile, 16 % in the highest, for the Colombia model), so plain accuracy by DI mixes a class effect with a dissimilarity effect. | Class confounding found in the first full run |

## MapBiomas labels — independent regrowth labels and experiments E1–E4 (extension)

This is a diagnostic extension, not part of the paper's method (`01c`, `02d`, `03f`). Labels come from **MapBiomas Colombia Collection 3** annual land cover, 1985–2024 (CC BY 4.0; cite "Proyecto MapBiomas Colombia – Colección 3"). The data are public COGs in `gs://mapbiomas-public/initiatives/colombia/collection_3/coverage/`.

**Sources for the rule.** The rule is taken from *Apéndice – Módulo de pérdida de vegetación y vegetación secundaria en Colección 3*, v1.0, October 2025. Its live URL (`colombia.mapbiomas.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2025/10/Apendice_modulo_perdida_de_vegetacion.pdf`) returns 404. The Wayback Machine snapshot of 2026-05-14 is stored in `data/raw/mapbiomas_docs/`. Two other documents were also checked: the C3 general appendix (`Apendice-1-Colombia-Coleccion-3.0.pdf`) and the C3 legend code sheet.

- **No ready-made product could be used.** MapBiomas shows Colombian secondary-vegetation and age maps on its platform, but the public bucket has no such layer under `initiatives/colombia/` (all 684 objects were listed on 2026-10-04). Only coverage, transitions (C1), fire and statistics are there, so the rule is re-implemented.
- **No Colombian accuracy figures are published.** The C3 appendix (§6) describes the validation design but gives no numbers. The "Análisis de exactitud" page describes MapBiomas Brazil. The vegetation-loss module says that its validation protocol is "in development".
